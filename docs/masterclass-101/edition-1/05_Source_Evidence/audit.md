## 6. Established recovery practice: accurate relationship

NIST SP 800-34 Rev. 1 places business impact analysis, recovery priorities, alternate strategies, roles, testing/training/exercises and maintenance inside contingency planning. RTO concerns how long a system resource can remain unavailable before unacceptable impact; RPO concerns the point in time to which data must be recovered. Neither alone proves fulfillment of a particular customer obligation. That last sentence is a reasoned teaching distinction, not a NIST quotation. Sections 3.2, 3.4 and 3.5 are the useful anchors. [N1]

NIST SP 800-184 connects recovery playbooks with mission/business priorities, dependencies, validation, metrics and continuous improvement. This supports teaching drills and after-action improvement. CONTINUITY Ω should be situated alongside that established practice, not advertised as the first approach to business continuity or as a replacement for incident response. Its distinctive course focus is the explicit promise, viable arrangement and scoped evidence. The 2016 document's historical five-function CSF wording must not be taught as the current CSF enumeration. [N2]

Neither source establishes that this course or CONTINUITY Ω is NIST-certified or comprehensively compliant.

CDC's quality-training guidance covers needs, measurable objectives, accurate content, engagement, usability/accessibility, evaluation, learner assessment and follow-up support. Apply those principles through: explicit beginner prerequisites and objectives; a repeated case with decisions; short practice and feedback; aligned assessments and answer rationales; readable bilingual resources; and a post-course application checklist. Use learner feedback and a later application task to improve the course. [P2]
