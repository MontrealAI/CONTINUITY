# CONTINUITY Ω MasterClass 101
## Teach, question and give useful feedback
Facilitator script, worked answers and formative assessment

## Run a focused 180-minute class

Prepare the learner guide, workbook and answer guide. Learners need a calculator and space to write; the case works offline. Keep the answers separate. Invite oral or typed responses and provide thinking time before asking for volunteers. Practice, feedback and assessment should serve the stated objectives. [P1]

**Orientation:** 0–2 minutes, ask what the customer must still receive after a disruption. At 2–6, learners write their starting hypothesis and choose roles. At 6–10, introduce the fictional case and distinguish a useful tabletop decision from actual business performance.

**Time boundary**
The twelve questions are distributed inside the lesson times. An optional pretest, repeated posttest or later follow-up adds time; it is not silently included in these 180 minutes.

## Keep one stable case throughout

Printer A buys the continuity service from the coordinator. Printer B supplies the alternate production. The original customer receives the labels; Morgan is its acceptance lead. The customer’s original invoice is outside the service ledger.

| Record | Authoritative teaching facts |
| --- | --- |
| K0 / P1 | 10,000 labels; artwork v3; matte white self-adhesive stock; 50 × 30 mm ±0.5 mm; readable approved code; damaged units excluded; no partial acceptance. |
| Place / deadline | Montréal warehouse, dock 3, M1; Thursday 12 November 2026, 16:00, America/Toronto; received and accepted by Morgan. |
| K1 / A-02 / C-01 | Checkpoint Tuesday 10 November 12:00: 8,000. Authorized amendment Tuesday 14:00: +2,000. Separate custodian log covers through Wednesday 11 November 12:00; no other changes. |
| K2 | 12,000 qualified units less 2,000 protected = 10,000. Extra simultaneous request: 5,000. |
| K3 | CONS-A, CONS-M, RES-B: simulated activation, limited data consent and 10,000-unit reservation; separate packet/release access. |
| K4 | Fee 5,600; complete cost 5,000; 4,700 due first; opening cash 1,200; advance 3,500 included in fee; conditional balance 2,100. |

K5 supplies failed R1 and an explicit 300 inspection invoice. K6 supplies a separate positive-control R2 from a fresh assumed starting state. The workbook prints all cards. Treat their claims as scenario assumptions; they do not authenticate any real customer, payment or product.

## Teach acceptance before recovery

**Objective:** learners write a promise that another person can use to accept or reject the result. Begin with the customer, not the failed application. [C1, pp. 7–8]

1. 0–3 minutes: read K0. Ask, “If the server restarts but the customer receives nothing, what succeeded?” Elicit the difference between a means and the accepted result.
2. 3–8: learners complete the Promise card. Ask the customer role to specify quantity, specification, clock, location and decision authority.
3. 8–12: exchange cards. Test one short shipment and one late shipment; revise unclear wording.
4. 12–15: answer Q01–Q02, reveal the explanations and ask one learner to justify a correction.

**Worked answer**
P1 requires 10,000 conforming labels, artwork v3 and the stated material/dimensions/quality, received and accepted by Morgan at M1 by 12 November 2026, 16:00 America/Toronto. Partial acceptance is excluded. A timestamped receipt, inspection/count record and authorized acceptance are required.

**If learners say “98% is close enough”**
Ask whether the original agreement permits that tolerance. It does not. A later compromise can be negotiated, but the original promise still failed. Record the new agreement as a new version, not a correction of history.

**Observe:** the card makes the rejection decidable. For participants’ own businesses, an unknown authority or deadline is a gap to resolve, not permission for the class to invent one.

## Turn an obstacle into a constructed route

**Objective:** identify the missing complements and specify a usable way to construct or obtain them. Continuity requires records, rights, capacity and coordination to work together. [C1, pp. 9–13]

1. 0–4 minutes: show the 8,000 checkpoint and A-02. Ask which source changes what is owed and why the cutoff matters.
2. 4–10: pairs build the Route card. Require a concrete output, owner, access route and completion check for each missing means.
3. 10–17: introduce the shared unavailable artwork login and extra 5,000-unit request. Have teams revise their route and capacity decision.
4. 17–20: answer Q03–Q04 and give corrective feedback.

| Problem | Worked construction |
| --- | --- |
| Missing amendment | Reconcile 8,000 + authorized 2,000 = 10,000; check C-01 coverage, origin and authority. |
| Shared unavailable login | Build an authorized current production packet and release channel accessible outside that failure. |
| Concurrent demand | 2,000 + 10,000 + 5,000 = 17,000 against 12,000. Decline or condition the extra request unless new qualified capacity is evidenced. |

**Coach for specificity**
“Find another supplier” is incomplete. Ask what that supplier must receive, be permitted to do and reserve. A useful answer may create a new packet, reservation protocol or coordination service; include the labor, cost and failure conditions of creating it.

Do not treat a matching checksum as proof that all accepted changes were captured.

## Make the offer worth accepting

**Objective:** connect each participant’s contribution, benefit and voluntary commitment. A technically possible route may still be unacceptable or commercially unworkable. [C1, pp. 11–13, 17–18]

1. 0–4 minutes: assign buyer, provider, customer, coordinator and reviewer roles. Read K3’s initial interest-only position.
2. 4–10: let each role say what it gives, needs and can refuse. Capture missing terms before revealing the K3 update.
3. 10–17: use CONS-A, CONS-M and RES-B to complete the arrangement. Ask the buyer to challenge price, advance and failure risk; accept a reasoned counteroffer or no-buy decision.
4. 17–20: answer Q05–Q06 and distinguish interest, commitment and usable capacity.

**Worked arrangement**
A buys a scoped continuity service from the coordinator; B commits the qualified 10,000-unit slot. The customer permits only the P1 artwork/specification/quantity/destination to B and the coordinator, without onward sharing. Copies are deleted seven days after case closure. Morgan retains acceptance authority. The packet and release channel work separately from the failed login.

The reviewer needs source access, a defined method and disclosed relationships/incentives. One person may hold multiple roles, but that limits the independence claim.

**Useful challenge**
“Why would A accept an advance that may pay nonrecoverable costs without full delivery?” Do not force agreement. Compare direct supplier purchase, different risk allocation, finance or no purchase. Changed terms require a new cost/cash check; willingness assumed in the case is not observed demand.

## Separate margin from available funds

**Objective:** calculate complete modeled cost, projected contribution and the payment-date gap without double-counting receipts. Use the six supplied costs; do not add the original customer invoice. [C1, pp. 17–19]

| Supplied modeled cost | CAD |
| --- | --- |
| Alternate production | 3,200 |
| Prepared and checked packet | 550 |
| Transport | 400 |
| Coordination and allocated overhead | 350 |
| Readiness test | 200 |
| Final inspection | 300 |
| Complete modeled cost | 5,000 |

**0–3 minutes:** let learners calculate before revealing totals. **3–8:** compare answers and ask which costs a real service ledger would still need to investigate. The case assumes this scope complete; real acquisition, internal time, failed trials, support, financing, insurance, refund duties and other items cannot be silently omitted.

**Worked arithmetic**
Fee 5,600 − cost 5,000 = projected contribution 600. The first five costs total 4,700 before acceptance. Opening uncommitted cash is 1,200. The original plan therefore lacks 3,500 before it can make those commitments.

**Misconception to repair**
“It makes 600, so it can pay.” Ask for the balance at the next payment date. Profitability on paper and timely funding are different conditions. The 600 is modeled, not realized profit.

## Close the gap without inventing money

At 8–11 minutes, reveal K4’s voluntarily agreed simulated advance and its restrictions. At 11–17, learners complete the favorable branch and challenge the terms. At 17–20, answer Q07–Q08. Then take the scheduled ten-minute break.

| Favorable teaching branch | Change | Cash |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening uncommitted funds |  | 1,200 |
| Advance received | +3,500 | 4,700 |
| Preacceptance costs paid | −4,700 | 0 |
| Full acceptance; balance received | +2,100 | 2,100 |
| Final inspection paid | −300 | 1,800 |

The advance is included in 5,600, not an extra 3,500 of revenue. Ending cash of 1,800 equals starting money of 1,200 plus modeled contribution of 600. This path requires full acceptance, receipt of the balance and payment of all 5,000 costs.

**Terms are part of the design**
The advance may offset authorized, documented nonrecoverable preacceptance costs; unspent funds are refundable. The balance is due only after full acceptance. A proposed advance is unavailable until received and usable under those terms. Do not assume every real buyer would accept this allocation.

K5 later creates an explicit failure invoice: 300 is due even after rejection. Its treatment is part of the failed branch, not an excuse to count the conditional 2,100. If learners propose financing or revised milestones, ask who agrees, when funds arrive and what obligations remain.

## Make the verification claim precise

**Objective:** separate evidence about records, readiness, outcomes and commerce; explain the limits of independence. [C1, pp. 13–14]

1. 0–4 minutes: ask what the supplied records establish and which real-world facts they do not establish.
2. 4–11: groups complete the claim table. Ask for an evidence ID and a boundary beside every favorable statement.
3. 11–18: another learner challenges one conclusion. Discuss raw sources, source custody, control of execution and organizational incentives.
4. 18–20: answer Q10. Keep Q09 for after the K5 capstone reveal.

| Claim at this stage | Defensible teaching answer |
| --- | --- |
| Reconstructed obligation | 10,000 within supplied K1 assumptions; real source authentication remains outside the class. |
| Fallback readiness | Conditional on the complete K2–K4 arrangement and stated assumptions; not observed physical execution. |
| Actual customer delivery | NOT ESTABLISHED; no actual goods exist in the course. |
| Real commercial value | NOT ESTABLISHED; case payments and cost scope are simulated. |

**Challenge the checker**
A separate code path can reduce some shared implementation risks; it does not by itself establish institutional independence. A paid reviewer should disclose relationships and incentives. Ask what source assertions remain trusted and what a fresh check would need.

Do not mark an unknown “FAILED” merely because evidence is absent. Conversely, a demonstrated shortfall is not just an unknown. These distinctions make the Passport useful to its next reader.

## Compare the best complete alternatives

**Objective:** design a comparison that could change a buyer’s decision. No measured route advantage is supplied by this course. [C1, pp. 19–20]

1. 0–3 minutes: nominate a competent conventional operations team as the baseline. Give it the same approved packet, provider, capacity and funding.
2. 3–9: pairs choose outcome, full-cost and effort measures. Include human rescue, setup, repeated attempts and remaining duties.
3. 9–14: challenge one unfair comparison and decide what evidence would support purchase, revision or no purchase.
4. 14–15: answer Q11 and state why the correct choice permits a conventional winner.

| Keep equivalent | Observe separately |
| --- | --- |
| Customer promise and difficulty | Correct accepted units, quality and time |
| Usable records, permissions and provider | Missing/duplicate obligations and interventions |
| Starting capacity, funds and access to help | Complete cost, staff time, construction and remaining duties |

**Worked answer**
“We will compare the conventional team and the proposed method on P1 using the same facts and resources. We will record accepted results, all effort and complete costs. An AI-assisted route that was not executed stays NOT RUN. A cheaper equally capable route wins.”

A baseline denied the amendment or qualified provider is not equally equipped. A software runtime alone does not price a complete service. A favorable estimated price may justify a next test; it does not establish measured superiority or actual willingness to pay.

## Run the capstone as a sequence of decisions

Have learners cover K5–K6. The exercise combines earlier work; it is not a surprise-memory test. Require a decision, owner and evidence at each stage. Role-play consent applies only within this tabletop.

| Capstone minute | Release / task | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–4 | Read P1; choose roles and scope | Unchanged acceptance conditions |
| 4–10 | K1–K3: construct route and exchange | Source coverage; shared login; 5,000 conflict |
| 10–15 | K4: price, terms and funding | Advance included in fee; genuine buyer choice |
| 15–22 | Release K5: adjudicate R1 | 9,800; balance withheld; 300 due |
| 22–28 | Optional K6: separate positive control | Fresh assumed state; do not erase R1 |
| 28–34 | Passport; peer rubric; one revision | Bounded claims and preserved liabilities |
| 34–35 | Q09 and one-sentence debrief | The next decision follows the evidence |

If time is tight, use minutes 22–28 to strengthen the R1 decision and mark R2 NOT RUN. Do not invent a favorable result to finish the worksheet. A team can rationally reject K4’s terms and retain an unfunded route; assess the reasoning.

**One helpful prompt at a time**
“What exactly blocks the next step?” Then: “What could you construct or negotiate to remove that block?” Finally: “Which evidence would let somebody else decide?” Avoid giving the whole answer before learners try.

## Preserve the failed result and its costs

**Customer outcome**
R1 records 9,800 conforming labels at 15:40. The time and specification do not cure the 200-unit shortfall. P1 requires 10,000 and forbids partial acceptance. Verdict: FAILED for the R1 tabletop customer outcome. Actual physical delivery remains NOT ESTABLISHED.

| R1 financial fact | Worked answer |
| --- | --- |
| Opening funds + advance | 1,200 + 3,500 = 4,700 available in the simulation. |
| Preacceptance costs paid | 4,700 paid; remaining cash = 0. |
| Conditional balance | 2,100 is not due or received without full acceptance. |
| Inspection failure invoice | 300 is due under K5’s explicit trigger; its funding is missing. |
| Advance/refund basis | K5 says the 3,500 funded documented eligible nonrecoverable costs. No unspent advance is shown; do not infer absence of all other duties. |

The team should hold further commitments, preserve the invoice and request an authorized resolution. It may propose a funded repair, negotiated settlement or another complete route. It must price and authorize that next action; no repair agreement is supplied.

**What not to repair on paper**
Do not reduce P1 to 9,800, book the full 5,600 fee, count the conditional 2,100 as available, or borrow R2’s hypothetical ending cash. Record remaining repair duties and uncertainty; the class does not invent a liability-free settlement.

A sound failure record is useful work. It identifies the unpaid 300 and the decision needed before more promises are made.

## Pass the exercise without overstating it

R2 is a **separate positive-control tabletop branch from a fresh assumed starting state**. It has its own simulated approval and mock receipt. It is not evidence that the actual R1 operation was repaired or rerun.

**Scoped outcome**
The new mock receipt records all 10,000 conforming labels accepted by Morgan at M1 at 15:40, before the 16:00 deadline. Another learner checks the supplied records. Verdict: DEMONSTRATED for the stated R2 tabletop acceptance claim, subject to its printed assumptions.

| R2 claim | Boundary |
| --- | --- |
| Modeled cash completion | 1,200 + 3,500 − 4,700 + 2,100 − 300 = 1,800. All 5,000 modeled costs paid. |
| Modeled contribution | 600; the other 1,200 of ending cash is starting money. |
| Actual physical delivery | NOT ESTABLISHED: the labels and receipt are simulated. |
| Real commercial value | NOT ESTABLISHED: no real buyer, cleared payment or observed complete cost. |
| Independence | Peer checking inside a class; disclose roles, method and trusted scenario facts. |

Keep R1’s failed verdict, costs, debt and unresolved duties alongside R2. Do not add the branch cash balances together. An actual successor run would need fresh consent, real available capacity and funds, and explicit treatment of the earlier obligations.

**Debrief question**
“What improved: the quality of our exercise record, the actual business outcome, or both?” Only the first has evidence in this classroom.

## Score the work, then improve it

Use 0 = absent or materially wrong; 1 = partly correct but missing a material element; 2 = the supported anchor below. Cite something in the learner’s work for each score. This course-designed rubric is unvalidated and does not certify operational readiness.

| Criterion | Anchor for 2 points |
| --- | --- |
| Promise | Preserves quantity, specification, deadline, place and authorized acceptance; no retrospective easing. |
| Constructed means | Reconciles amendment/cutoff; supplies a permitted packet and access route; resolves the capacity conflict. |
| Voluntary arrangement | Names buyer/seller/provider/beneficiary; records consent, reservation, price and failure/refund terms. |
| Cost and cash | Shows 5,000 cost, 3,500 initial gap, included advance, conditional 2,100, R1’s unpaid 300 and separate R2 cash. |
| Evidence and verdict | R1 fails; R2 is scoped tabletop only; real delivery/market remain unestablished; checker limits are disclosed. |
| Comparison and next decision | Uses a capable equally equipped baseline, counts complete effort and proposes an evidence-based next action. |

**Mandatory correction before closing**
Ask for revision if the learner erases R1, treats the advance as extra revenue, counts the 2,100 after rejection, or calls mock delivery/payments real. A high total does not cancel these errors.

Peer and facilitator scores may differ. Discuss the evidence rather than treating the number as objective truth. Record one specific revision and, if useful, a revised score. Report knowledge-check and performance scores separately; do not combine them into a resilience rating.

## Transfer a capability, then test its value

**Objective:** propose a distinct next use and a comparison that could establish the retained capability’s contribution. Reuse, repeat demand and an improved discovery process are different claims. [C1, p. 21]

1. 0–4 minutes: name an artifact or method worth retaining and its rights, costs and known limits.
2. 4–9: apply it to a different promise. Ask what must change and how a competent team without that retained capability would approach the same task.
3. 9–13: complete a specific next action, owner, evidence requirement and review date. Invite one changed starting hypothesis.
4. 13–15: answer Q12, collect one useful course improvement and close.

**Worked example**
Retain the versioned obligation format and source-review procedure. Test them with a tool-rental company honoring bookings after a scheduling outage. Define the new accepted result, permissions and capacity rules. Compare with and without the retained method using equivalent starting information and resources; include adaptation, requalification and human effort.

No successor was executed in this course. Mark the proposed test NOT RUN. A second sale of the same label service would instead inform repeat demand; it would not alone establish that the retained method enables a different task.

**Constructive next step**
A learner may draft one real promise card or identify its acceptance owner. Actual activation still needs fresh permission, sources, capacity and funding. A completed course form is not authority to act.

## Answers 1–4

### Q01 · C

**Morgan receives and accepts 10,000 conforming v3 labels at site M1 by the deadline.** The promise concerns the customer’s accepted result: quantity, specification, place and time. A restart or dispatch may support the route but does not establish receipt and acceptance. In this course, even a matching mock receipt establishes only the tabletop result.

### Q02 · A

**Failed against P1; record any new agreement separately.** The original acceptance conditions remain authoritative. A later compromise or a new run may be useful, but it cannot change the historical result. The shortfall is 200 labels.

### Q03 · B

**10,000, after checking the amendment’s authority and coverage through the cutoff.** 8,000 + 2,000 = 10,000. The amendment matters because it changes what is owed. Source origin, authority and completeness through the agreed cutoff still need checking; a matching checksum does not establish those facts.

### Q04 · B

**Build and test an authorized, separately accessible current packet and release channel.** A second supplier does not remove a shared dependency. The useful intervention creates a permitted, current and usable way to obtain and release the right work. Its construction, rights and cost belong in the service.

## Answers 5–8

### Q05 · C

**Total demand is 17,000 against 12,000; both cannot receive unconditional coverage.** Protected work and simultaneous promises share the same resource. The new request adds a 5,000-unit shortfall. A qualified additional slot, a voluntarily agreed priority rule or a declined request may solve it; silently double-booking does not.

### Q06 · A

**Interest; a usable reservation and its terms still need confirmation.** Willingness, authorized commitment, usable capacity and collected payment are different evidence states. The parties must voluntarily settle the product, window, rights, price, activation and failure terms.

### Q07 · B

**CAD 600.** 5,600 − 5,000 = 600. Ending cash of 1,800 on the favorable teaching branch includes 1,200 of starting money. Neither calculation is observed profit or real market evidence.

### Q08 · C

**The gap is CAD 3,500; CAD 2,100 remains conditional on full acceptance.** The advance closes this modeled gap only when received and available under the agreed terms. It is part of the fee, not extra revenue. Restrictions, refund duties and payment conditions remain relevant.

## Answers 9–12

### Q09 · A

**P1 failed in the tabletop; do not count the conditional CAD 2,100; record the unpaid inspection and next decision.** A mock receipt of 9,800 does not meet the 10,000-unit promise. K5 makes the CAD 300 inspection charge due despite failure; its funding is missing. Actual goods, actual payment and real commercial value remain not established.

### Q10 · B

**Disclose the relationship, method, source custody and limits; assess independence for the particular claim.** Independent checking has several dimensions. Separate code, protected source evidence, control of execution and organizational incentives are not interchangeable. A reviewer’s report must identify what was checked and what remains trusted or unknown.

### Q11 · C

**Use the same promise, usable records and resources; count complete costs and human work; allow a conventional winner.** A capable conventional route is the relevant baseline. An AI-assisted route must actually run before its performance is claimed. Quote, modeled estimate and measured result must remain distinguishable; no purchase can be the rational choice.

### Q12 · A

**A fresh distinct task compared with and without that capability under equivalent resources, including adaptation costs.** Retention creates a candidate capability, not proof of its contribution. The next use needs its own promise, rights, funding and evidence. Repeat demand, successor enablement and improvement of the discovery process are different claims.

## Make the learning useful after the class

Give one point per correct knowledge-check answer, maximum 12. Keep the first responses and any corrected responses separate. Use the pattern of errors to choose the next explanation or exercise. The score describes responses to these twelve questions; it does not measure business resilience.

**A practical feedback conversation**
Name the decision the learner made well, point to its evidence, identify one material gap, and ask for a revised action. For example: “Your capacity calculation protects the existing 2,000. Your funding line still counts 2,100 before acceptance. What can be committed with the cash actually available?”

For self-study, cover the answers, complete each workbook task, then compare your reasoning with the key. Use K5 before K6. If working alone, record the absence of an independent peer check rather than inventing one.

Ask learners which preparation step they intend to use, what may prevent it and what support they need. A later review can examine whether that work was applied. End-of-course proficiency alone does not establish learning gain; satisfaction alone does not establish effectiveness. [P2]

**Optional follow-up — outside the 180 minutes**
Agree a later review date. Examine a permitted, anonymized promise card or decision record; ask what changed, what evidence was obtained and what remains blocked. New real-world action still needs its own approvals and resources. A repeated quiz or transfer review is optional extra time.

Record attendance or course completion only as such. This course issues no operational certification, accreditation, safety approval or guarantee.
