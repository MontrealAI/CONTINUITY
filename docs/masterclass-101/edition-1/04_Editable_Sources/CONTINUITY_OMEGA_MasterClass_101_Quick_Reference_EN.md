## The continuity decision

Start with a customer promise whose failure matters. Establish a usable way to deliver it and an arrangement that willing participants can carry out. Judge the result against the agreed promise. [C1; F2]

| Step | The question to answer |
| --- | --- |
| 1  Promise | What output, quantity, quality, place and deadline will the named customer authority accept? |
| 2  Missing means | Which unavailable record, permission, capability or dependency blocks delivery? What must be constructed? |
| 3  Arrangement | Who agrees to provide what, on which terms? What capacity is actually reserved, including simultaneous demand? |
| 4  Money | What does the complete service cost? Is money available before each payment, including failure obligations? |
| 5  Verification | What evidence would establish or disprove the outcome? Who checks it, with what independence? |
| 6  Comparison | Could a capable conventional option meet the same promise under the same conditions? |
| 7  Next use | What reusable capability remains? Does it help on a different task after transfer costs? Preserve failures too. |

### State each claim separately

| Claim | Evidence you need |
| --- | --- |
| Outcome | The required output, actually received and accepted within the frozen conditions. |
| Service feasibility | Consenting participants, usable capacity, permissions, complete costs, timely funds and workable obligations. |
| Market evidence | Real independent buying, accepted value, collections and complete costs. Repeated demand supports repeatability. |

Give a scoped verdict: supported, failed, or not established. Name the tested setting: tabletop, digital product, physical delivery or real transaction. A completed form does not prove delivery.

## The cash timing check

Fictional teaching case. The continuity service fee is CAD 5,600. Complete modeled cost is CAD 5,000, leaving a projected contribution of CAD 600. Before acceptance, CAD 4,700 is due but only CAD 1,200 is available. The initial funding gap is CAD 3,500.

### Funded positive-control branch

| Event | Cash change | Available cash |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Starting money | — | 1,200 |
| Receive advance | +3,500 | 4,700 |
| Pay before acceptance | −4,700 | 0 |
| Receive balance after full acceptance | +2,100 | 2,100 |
| Pay final inspection | −300 | 1,800 |

**The reconciliation**
The advance and balance total 5,600. Ending cash of 1,800 contains the original 1,200 plus the 600 contribution. The advance is part of the fee. The original customer’s order invoice is outside this service ledger.

### When the receipt says 9,800

The promise requires 10,000, with no authorized short shipment. The failed tabletop branch cannot collect the acceptance-dependent 2,100 balance. After the 4,700 payments, cash is zero; the case’s 300 inspection invoice remains unpaid. Record that obligation and any unresolved repair duty. The separate positive-control branch does not erase or fund this failure.

### Your first useful action

Choose one commitment, name its owner and acceptance authority, and draft the promise card. Gather the existing recovery plan and a credible alternative. Identify the first missing fact or capability and arrange a bounded tabletop with willing participants. Use the workbook to preserve decisions and the facilitator guide to challenge them.

Course edition 1.0. All amounts and case events above are invented. Your real case needs its own complete cost scope, actual cash timing, consent, failure terms and evidence.
